# FLASH redesign experiment — sealed 2026-10-31

This experiment is preserved for evidence and reuse, not as an approved system
to extend or deploy. Branch: `docs/flash-redesign`. Archive tag:
`flash-redesign-sealed-20261031`. The previous experiment was
`proto/show-and-servo`, sealed at `80d90b783` in
[PR #78](https://github.com/TheWisp/lerobot/pull/78).

## Why it is sealed

The tracker, demo preparation, pose arbitration and editor accumulated separate
rules before the underlying object model and evidence contract were settled.
The editor became difficult to use. A working debugger and occasional successful
robot trials did not establish reliable tracking through changed views,
occlusion, grasp or release. Passing software tests did not establish pose
accuracy. The next effort starts with tracker research and a minimal inspection
tool; the old proposed architecture is historical, not an implementation mandate.

## What actually runs

```text
Current RGB-D + SAM mask → current measured partial surface
Consecutive-image matches + depth → proposed rigid motion
Fixed reference surface + motion proposal → mask/depth pose fitting
Object-point and scene-group tracks → pose prediction and disagreement checks
Optional robot attachment layer → gripper-backed prediction/correction
Selected timestamped pose → keyframe policy → independent motor loop
```

The observation is measured from the current image. Predicted group points do
not fill in missing sensor geometry. UFM correspondences initialize the surface
fit; their identities are not constraints in its final optimization. The causal
service separately propagates object/group motion and compares disputed poses
against the same image. This is a mixture of initialization, pose selection and
fallback, not one joint estimator of surface and point-motion evidence.

The ordinary surface tracker retains its first partial view. The final archived
experiment lets a prepared object with multiple reviewed views fit their combined
surface, registered into that same fixed coordinate system. It does not grow a
model automatically. No physical trial validated that final change, and the
main insertion demo's proposed extra view was not approved. Manual reference
selection is not the intended model-growth architecture.

## What is worth retaining

- Ordinary LeRobot episodes, capture timestamps, calibration and recorded
  evidence; a fixed-rate motor loop separate from expensive perception.
- Shared perception components, current-mask/depth surface checks, debugging
  views, and regression tests. Reuse depends on testing their contracts.
- The [benchmark](../../../../../benchmarks/FLASH_TRACKING.md): nine YCB clips,
  ten Caryx clips and two local manipulation demos. They are development cases,
  not an unseen holdout. Local demos lack independent pose ground truth.
- The [failure ledger](../../../../../docs/proofs/flash-contact-observation/FAILURE_LEDGER.md),
  [surface-map investigation](../../../../../docs/proofs/flash-surface-multiview/README.md)
  and other dated evidence. Low surface residual, available pose, and task
  success are different measurements; none substitutes for the others.

## Agreed direction and unresolved choices

Use SAM object membership, valid sensor depth and co-moving point tracks in one
shared tracker. Gradually accumulate supported measured surfaces in a stable
object frame. Boundary trimming needs measurement: it removes contamination
but can also erase useful support. Do not merge geometry when its alignment is
uncertain. Automatic rollback of already merged geometry was not agreed and is
not a requirement for the first implementation.

Measure whether point motion should initialize fitting, remain a constraint
during fitting, or provide fallback only. Distinguish points on the object from
a separate support group that is only hypothesized to move with it. Neither
predicted points nor repeated use of the same pixels are fresh measurements.
Define when model growth is trustworthy and test bad accepted alignments, not
only loss/rejection. New-side registration remains an unsolved problem.

Robot/gripper relationships belong above the robot-independent tracker. Research
a small interface for optional, timestamped motion or relation hypotheses that
can be contradicted or withdrawn. External RGB-D clips must exercise the core
without robot state. The tracker research needs object selection, playback and
evidence inspection; a skill editor is not a prerequisite.
