# Ten new RGB-D tracking clips, before changing the estimator

The preceding [dish audit](../surface-audit/README.md) found a visible dish rejected when its surface disagreement reached 5.128 mm against a 5 mm gate, while its environment fallback had 11 agreeing points but required 12. This sweep records that case as a baseline and asks whether other scenes fail in the same way. **No tracking rule was changed for this sweep.**

## Inputs and what the numbers mean

We selected ten previously unused episodes from [CaryxAI/everyday-manipulation-3d](https://huggingface.co/datasets/CaryxAI/everyday-manipulation-3d) before inspecting their FLASH results. They span 3,002 RGB-D frames and 14 annotated object roles. The source snapshot was `3c2c3bca2283b3e4b3953063d2971e668ebb9661`; FLASH code was `c183227b23`. All ten share one iPhone LiDAR/ARKit source and moving camera. RGB and depth were imported on the same rays, with each episode's own depth intrinsics. Source masks were used for the first pass; a second pass used SAM3 name prompts. The supplied masks are annotations, not guaranteed perfect object surfaces.

Each episode used the same saved processing configuration as the three earlier external clips: 5 mm pose and group fit limits, 12 group inliers. The configuration is retained in each local dataset under `flash/v1/observation-config.json`; no parameters were tuned to these ten cases. The applicable camera-axis conversion was recorded in each imported `source.json`. Although the source dataset card describes camera poses as aligned with the shipped images, an explicit quarter-turn was empirically supported by background reprojection for some episodes, as in the [earlier camera audit](../THREE_SCENE_RETEST.md). That source/convention discrepancy remains unresolved. The [background reprojection audit](camera_reprojection.json) gives a median below 2.1 pixels in the first 90 frames of nine clips; episode 2 is 5.84 pixels, so its later failures have an additional camera-consistency confound. The audit assumes tracked, unmasked background features remain still; it is not camera ground truth.

**O** means a directly accepted RGB-D pose, **P** means a pose predicted from object points or an environment relationship, and **L** means no pose. Counts include only frames where the supplied object mask contains pixels. A prediction is not an accuracy label. These episodes provide no independent object-pose ground truth, so this measures availability, surface agreement, mask agreement, and visible failure patterns; it does **not** measure millimetre or degree accuracy against truth.

## Repeated patterns

1. **A mask can cover pixels whose measured depth belongs to something else.** The filtered first-frame surface disagrees with all raw depth inside the same mask by 74.5 mm for the mug, 157.8 mm for the pouring bottle, 95.6 mm for the spoon and 163.4 mm for the pale dish, measured at the 90th percentile. The first frame is nevertheless published as a reference; later frames face the 5 mm publication gate. The code constructs its reference from filtered points but computes that gate against _all_ masked surface points. These numbers expose an inconsistent initialization/acceptance contract; they are not pose errors. Concavity, a thin reflective tool, transparency, or a mask crossing another surface can all produce this pattern. We have not isolated one cause for each object.
2. **Transparency can return background depth rather than an invalid pixel.** Every pixel of the supplied jar mask has a nonzero depth in the median frame, but the late RGB image shows the tabletop through the glass. At frame 173, a [reproducible local plane check](glass_depth.py) finds a 1.58 mm median absolute residual in nearby tabletop pixels and a 2.12 mm median residual _inside_ the jar mask; 71% of masked pixels lie within 5 mm of that table plane. The raw depth image therefore supplies little direct evidence for the glass wall, despite 100% nominal depth validity. This is a local plane approximation, not glass ground truth; [the numbers](glass_depth.json) depend on the sampled tabletop. The SAM3 jar mask also overlaps its supplied mask by only .42 median, so segmentation compounds the problem. The separate lid stays available throughout.
3. **One rigid pose is the wrong output for deforming material.** The cloth is lost on 447/455 source-visible frames even with supplied masks, and SAM3 agrees with those masks at .96 median. Shoelaces are lost on 250/318 supplied-mask frames. In the SAM3 shoelaces pass, the fit crashes in `ConvexHull` because projected support is collinear. This is a distinct robustness defect; the pass has no complete score. A global rigid pose cannot represent a folded garment or changing lace shape.
4. **Small or narrow visible regions often lack a stable full pose.** The charger, spoon and partially covered mug have many explicit `visible patch is too narrow for a full rigid pose` rejections. Their masks can remain visible while the measured patch cannot constrain all rotations. The system should not pretend the unmeasured rotations are accurate; losing every useful position or local landmark is also unnecessarily coarse.
5. **Segmentation identity can fail independently of fitting.** In episode 7 the supplied brush mask includes small patches on the hand; the SAM3 `small brush` result overlaps the supplied brush mask by only .07 median. The pale dish in episode 90 is never detected by the `white square bowl` text prompt. Those cannot be repaired by changing the pose threshold.
6. **A continuously published pose need not be stable.** Episode 53's plate remains visually in place while its published world pose varies by 11.85 mm translation and 7.12° orientation at the median relative to frame 0 (p90 23.64 mm, 12.74°). The clip lacks external plate-pose truth, and camera consistency is about 2.09 pixels median, so this is a stationarity warning rather than a calibrated pose-error measurement. It shows why a high O+P count alone is an inadequate success measure.

Across the supplied-mask pass, 419 of 3,632 source-visible object-frames have directly observed poses, 924 have predicted poses, and 2,289 have none. These totals combine rigid, deformable, reflective and transparent cases; they are a workload description, not a leaderboard score. Predictions must be tested for correctness before acting on them.

## What published work adds

- [ClearPose](https://progress.robotics.umich.edu/projects/clearpose/) and [RGB-D Local Implicit Function](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.00622) report that ordinary RGB-D sensors do not reliably measure transparent-object surfaces; learned completion or additional evidence is needed. Our jar depth check is a local instance of that failure mode, not proof that a particular completion model would solve it.
- [BundleSDF](https://bundlesdf.github.io/) keeps a pool of previously posed views to track and rebuild unknown rigid objects under viewpoint changes and occlusion. That is a relevant alternative to judging every new view against only our first partial surface; its reported near-real-time rate is about 10 Hz, not a proven drop-in for our 30 Hz robot loop.
- [DynamicFusion](https://www.cv-foundation.org/openaccess/content_cvpr_2015/html/Newcombe_DynamicFusion_Reconstruction_and_2015_CVPR_paper.html) tracks local surface deformation in RGB-D rather than assigning one rigid transform to everything. Its paper also reports failures with large motion and occluded regions, so non-rigid support still needs uncertainty and recovery.
- The [CDTB RGB-D tracking benchmark](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ICCV_2019/html/Lukezic_CDTB_A_Color_and_Depth_Visual_Object_Tracking_Dataset_and_ICCV_2019_paper.html) found RGB-only trackers could outperform then-current RGB-D trackers on 2D object tracking. That does not rank 6D pose methods, but it reinforces that an available depth number should not automatically dominate RGB tracking.

The next controlled comparisons should test **surface membership and measurement validity**, **motion/landmark continuity**, and **deformation representation** as separate questions. Raising the 5 mm gate across all scenes would confound them: it could increase availability while accepting wrong poses. The dish, transparent jar, cloth and visually stationary plate are now distinct regression cases for judging any proposed change.
