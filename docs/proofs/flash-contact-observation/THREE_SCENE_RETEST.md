# External RGB-D perception retest

Three complete human-manipulation episodes from [CaryxAI/everyday-manipulation-3d](https://huggingface.co/datasets/CaryxAI/everyday-manipulation-3d) were imported as **actionless** LeRobot observations and processed with the same FLASH surface-pose and causal scene-group path. Their supplied per-object masks isolate pose tracking from segmentation quality. These clips have camera motion but no robot/gripper fallback; a reported pose is not an accuracy label. Source images and videos remain in the local dataset cache.

| Scene and local dataset                   | Camera check                                                                                   | Object: observed / predicted / lost frames         |
| ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Small device into white bowl, 143 frames  | Source-axis reprojection median 20.0 px; explicit 90° basis conversion 1.7 px                  | device: **51 / 92 / 0**; bowl: **9 / 7 / 127**     |
| Lid and glass jar, 154 frames             | 16.3 px → 1.1 px with the same explicit basis conversion                                       | lid: **113 / 41 / 0**; jar: **9 / 82 / 63**        |
| Green bottle onto white plate, 194 frames | Source axes already agree: 2.0 px median; applying the quarter-turn would worsen it to 12.4 px | bottle: **102 / 90 / 2**; plate: **50 / 103 / 41** |

“Observed” means a new RGB-D surface fit passed the configured checks; “predicted” means a prior/group estimate was carried forward; “lost” means no usable pose was published. None of these counts alone proves millimetre or angular correctness. The source camera-pose feature and the aligned RGB-D feature are preserved separately in the first two derived datasets. At frames 0, middle and last, the aligned imports read back **identical RGB and depth** to their original imports, and the stored raw pose matches the original exactly; only the explicit pose basis differs. The third episode uses no basis conversion. All three evidence manifests completed in source order.

The camera comparison is not based solely on our imported videos. Independent RGB feature matches on the **original** bottle/plate source at frame 15 give 0.37 px median reprojection error using its recorded camera pose and 7.39 px if the quarter-turn is wrongly applied; at frame 60 the values are 0.95 and 15.31 px. This is a negative control for the conversion used on the other two episodes.

We also ran the **same pipeline with SAM3-generated masks** rather than the supplied masks, using a first-frame point inside each supplied object mask. Median overlap with the supplied masks and resulting pose availability were:

| Scene          | SAM3 mask overlap, median IoU | Observed / predicted / lost with SAM3 masks    |
| -------------- | ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Device / bowl  | 0.940 / 0.982                 | device **50 / 90 / 3**; bowl **9 / 20 / 114**  |
| Lid / jar      | 0.365 / 0.000                 | lid **2 / 80 / 72**; jar **0 / 0 / 154**       |
| Bottle / plate | 0.930 / 0.982                 | bottle **93 / 95 / 6**; plate **42 / 149 / 3** |

SAM3 tracked the masks well in the opaque bowl and bottle/plate scenes, but close lid and transparent jar were merged into the lid role and the jar role had no mask. Prompting the jar **alone** produced a mask, yet its median overlap with the supplied jar mask was only 0.455; this is not just a two-role bookkeeping error. Conversely, the plate's supplied and SAM3 masks overlap at 0.982 median, yet the lost-frame count changes from 41 to 3. That status sensitivity to small mask changes is a reason to test pose quality separately, not to infer better tracking from availability alone. Each local dataset keeps this comparison under `flash/v1/observation-evidence-sam3/episode_000000`; the jar dataset also has `observation-evidence-sam3-jar-only`. The supplied-mask evidence remains the normal replay.

The [follow-up surface and name audit](surface-audit/README.md) separates these causes further. The point-prompt adapter treated the second click inside the first broad mask as refinement, explaining the missing jar role. Actual text detection with `lid` and `glass jar` gives a separate lid mask but still includes the lid in the jar mask. The object named “white bowl” by the source is visually a shallow square dish. Its first SAM-run pose loss is at frame 13: the surface error exceeds the configured gate while the fallback group, eroded to 12 identities, cannot fit all 12 within that gate. The audit reconstructs the actual surfaces, tests alternative fits, and replays the group points.

The failures are concrete:

- The bowl stays plainly visible. At frame 30 its mask contains 5,989 pixels and 4,148 usable depth points, yet the proposed surface disagrees with measured depth by 8.6 mm at the 90th percentile, above this configuration's 5 mm gate. The group has only 12 surviving points and the accepted pose is lost from frame 22 onward. Camera-frame alignment did not solve surface acceptance.
- The jar has a transparent body and severe depth ambiguity. At frame 120 its mask contains 1,610 pixels and 881 depth points, but the proposal is rejected as an insufficiently constrained rigid view; the depth residual is 45 mm. It remains lost through the end even though the mask reappears. The same threshold failure also occurs on opaque objects, so transparency is not the sole cause.
- The bottle loses its pose at frames 88–89 during handling, then reacquires it at frame 90. Its mask still has about 1,400 pixels during the gap: this is a fit/group dropout, not a missing segmentation. The plate is lost over parts of frames 97–157 while the bottle and hand cross it, and returns to an observed pose at frame 159. A visible target mask does not ensure a verified pose.

We tested **only as a sidecar** raising both the surface and group fit tolerances from 5 to 10 mm, without changing normal evidence or defaults. It changed the bowl to 125 observed / 18 predicted / 0 lost, jar to 36 / 76 / 42, and plate to 182 / 12 / 0. This is not an accuracy improvement claim: the apparently stationary bowl's estimated pose origin moved as much as 18 mm from its first pose and the plate's as much as 23 mm. With the normal setting, the bowl had only 16 available frames, so its smaller 6.7 mm maximum is not a fair full-clip comparison. A larger gate trades abstention for uncertain poses; it should not be selected from availability counts alone. The next test needs an independently checked static-object trajectory or contact geometry and a sensor-noise model, not a case-specific threshold.

For a second sensor and independent object-pose labels, the existing [YCBInEOAT checks](../flash-external-demo/README.md) remain useful controls. Re-scoring their saved evidence gives 30/30 available sugar-box motion poses at 1.8 mm median surface displacement error and 1.1° median orientation error (about 64% of labeled points visible). On a more occluded cracker-box sequence (about 29% visible), the same path abstains on all 30 subsequent frames; rejected raw fits are grossly wrong. Those are 31-frame fixed-camera clips, not proof of long-horizon occlusion recovery.

The reproducible camera basis is [`caryx_pose_camera_from_rgbd_camera.txt`](caryx_pose_camera_from_rgbd_camera.txt). Use it only for a source episode whose own frame check supports it. The importer accepts it with `--pose-camera-from-rgbd-camera`; without that argument it retains the source pose unchanged. The complete per-frame estimate, proposal, mask and group evidence is in each local dataset's `flash/v1/observation-evidence/episode_000000` directory. The 10 mm sensitivity results remain separate under `/tmp/flash_caryx_threshold_sensitivity/`.

Local dataset names under `~/.cache/huggingface/lerobot/demos/` are `flash_external_caryx_device_bowl_aligned`, `flash_external_caryx_lid_jar_aligned` and `flash_external_caryx_bottle_plate_observation`. Their earlier uncorrected counterparts remain available for comparison.
