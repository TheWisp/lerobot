# Are the apparent point failures tracker slip?

The moving YCBInEOAT clips **do not have independently annotated material-point tracks**. The benchmark seeds points from first-frame measured depth, transports them with the dataset's annotated per-frame object pose, and projects them into later RGB images ([reference builder](../../../benchmarks/flash_surface_ycbineoat.py)). Later masks and depth only decide whether each projected point is eligible; they do not check that the annotation follows the same image feature. Therefore an apparent point error can be caused by the tracker, the pose annotation, the depth seed, or image/pose alignment.

To check the two frames previously called surface slip, I tracked each eligible reference pixel **one RGB frame forward** with OpenCV pyramidal Lucas–Kanade optical flow (`winSize=(21,21)`, `maxLevel=3`). I retained only points eligible and predicted visible in both frames with a forward–backward flow error below 1 px. The table gives medians over those same retained points. A difference compares displacement **vectors** per point, not just their magnitudes.

| Moving holdout transition                     | Points | RGB flow motion | TAPNext motion | Pose-reference motion | Flow–TAPNext difference | Flow–reference difference |
| --------------------------------------------- | -----: | --------------: | -------------: | --------------------: | ----------------------: | ------------------------: |
| Bleach, local frames 26→27 (1.70→1.76 s)      |     28 |         7.40 px |        7.57 px |               1.44 px |                 0.42 px |                   7.01 px |
| Cracker box, local frames 15→16 (0.51→0.53 s) |      6 |         0.84 px |        0.75 px |               7.70 px |                 0.42 px |                   7.37 px |
| Sugar box, local frames 19→20 (control)       |     30 |         2.32 px |        2.49 px |               3.36 px |                 0.26 px |                   1.11 px |

At these two hard transitions, the image flow and TAPNext move together while the pose-derived reference jumps relative to them. Several other trackers make a similar displacement on the bleach transition. This is **strong evidence that local reference inconsistency contributes to the measured mismatch**, not proof that TAPNext kept the exact same material points. Lucas–Kanade can also follow the wrong patch on a smooth or occluded surface; the small cracker sample especially needs caution. A ±1-frame reference-shift check did not reveal a consistent global offset across the clips, so the evidence points more toward local pose annotation error than a universal timestamp shift. The previously reported 8.5/12.2 px mismatch frames and later apparent recovery cannot be labelled confirmed tracker slip from this reference alone.

The recorded object outline provides a separate, coarse image check. Between those same frames, the bleach mask centroid moves `[+6.50, -1.18]` px, close to TAPNext's median `[+7.55, -0.50]` px motion, versus the pose reference's `[+0.68, +1.20]` px. The cracker mask centroid moves `[-0.32, +0.98]` px, close to TAPNext's `[-0.56, +0.28]` px, versus the pose reference's `[+0.33, +7.66]` px. Translating each first-frame mask by the candidate median displacement gives next-frame mask intersection-over-union of 0.890 (tracker) versus 0.696 (reference) for bleach, and 0.867 versus 0.738 for cracker. These masks are dataset annotations and may themselves have error; centroid motion and translation-only overlap do not establish a full 6D pose or material-point identity. They do show that **overall image shape sides with the observed/tracked displacement on these transitions**.

The benchmark's displayed dots are intentionally **raw** tracker output; its mask/depth checks are diagnostics, not feedback that moves the dots. FLASH's direct RGB-D pose backend does compare a matched pose with the segmented depth surface and can try a shape-only fallback. Its fast causal point path samples aligned depth at tracked pixels and fits rigid motion, but does not use the whole current silhouette or depth shape to correct that motion on each frame. Thus the raw-dot comparison isolates the point frontend, while a separate end-to-end evaluation is still needed for the grounded pose used by the robot. Even there, a smooth or symmetric visible surface can support several different material-point alignments, so outline/depth agreement cannot by itself certify point identity.

The [exact-motion smooth/textured control](SYNTHETIC.md) does isolate one real factor: TAPNext 2-px coverage drops from 0.995 on the textured rotating plane to 0.699 on the otherwise identical smooth plane, and Lucas–Kanade drops from 0.996 to 0.378. Weak texture can make material-point tracking ambiguous. The cracker clip is also partly hand-occluded. Neither observation apportions the actual errors on these two real transitions among texture, occlusion and annotation. Independent material-point labels or trusted synchronized pose measurements on a real smooth-object clip are needed before ranking trackers for FLASH by their YCBInEOAT 2-px scores.
